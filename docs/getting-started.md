# Getting started

What a new user hands over before the system can run, and why each item is needed. The guided way to provide all of it is [onboarding](onboarding.md): your Muse asks for each item in order. This page is the reference list behind that conversation.

## Your resume

A current resume, as a PDF. Every application uses a version tailored from this file, and nothing on an application may come from anywhere else. If a fact is not on your resume or in your standing answers, the system does not put it on a form.

## The standing facts

Application forms ask the same questions hundreds of times. The system keeps one answer set and reuses it:

- Legal name, and your name in your native language if you have one. Some forms ask for it directly.
- Email, phone, and home address, including ZIP code. Some portals refuse to save a profile without one.
- Work authorization: citizenship, residency status, and whether you need sponsorship, in the exact wording forms should get.
- Education: each school, degree, years attended, and grade point average.
- Employers and job titles, exactly as they appear on your resume, with dates.
- Notice period: how much time you need before starting a new job.
- Equal opportunity answers: race, gender, disability status, veteran status. Forms ask; the system answers the same way every time.
- Two standing choices: whether it may invent a fun fact when a form asks for one, and whether it may agree to AI notetakers recording your interviews.

## Your targets

- The job titles you want. Titles decide the searches, so include the variants employers actually use.
- Locations: cities or states you would work in, and whether fully remote roles count.
- Pay floors: the lowest pay you will accept for an onsite or hybrid role, and for a remote role. Postings that publish a range are judged on the top of the range.
- Any work you do not want under any title. In this setup, consulting work and reporting-only work are out.

## Your block list

Companies you will never apply to, for any reason. The system declines them on sight and never re-checks the decision. You can add to the list at any time with one message.

## Your reply rules

Recruiters will write back. Decide in advance:

- When the system may accept a call, and what times you are free for calls.
- What it may share without asking: here, work authorization yes, phone number only inside an active role conversation, salary numbers never.
- Standing answers to common questions. Here: no relocation help is fine, an on-site interview is fine.
- What must come back to you. Anything outside the rules you set lands in your chat for a decision.

## Your accounts

- LinkedIn, with Premium Career active. Used for job alerts, connection invitations, and replies. Your Muse signs in as you in its browser.
- LinkedIn notification emails turned on (job alerts, messages, invitations). The system reads that mail to discover jobs and hear about new messages. Step by step: docs/linkedin-notifications.md.
- Jobright, on a paid plan. One of the two main sources of new postings.
- Indeed. The second source. Indeed is used for finding and screening only; applications always go in on the employer's own site.
- Gmail, with Google Calendar and Google Tasks connected. Confirmations and recruiter mail land in Gmail. Interviews go on the calendar. Interview requests and assessments become tasks.

## Your two daily windows

Pick two times a day when you can spend a few minutes on your phone: approving sign-ins and sends, and solving captchas. In this setup they are 1:30 PM and 7:30 PM. Work that needs you waits for those windows instead of interrupting you at random.

## What happens next

Your Muse fills in the two config files, shows them to you, and starts with a small run so you can watch the screening decisions before the volume goes up. Every rule can be changed later with one message. When you change a rule, Muse updates it everywhere it is written and confirms the change back to you.
